# Dimensions and scoring

Same seven dimensions, same order. Scores are whole numbers from 1
to 10. Higher is better. Use `NR` when evidence is insufficient, with
the reason. Do not use zero for missing evidence.

| Dimension | What it assesses | 3 weak | 5 mixed | 7 solid | 9 excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Utility | Value of the bounded result to the intended user | Weak fit or disproportionate burden | Plausible benefit with substantial friction | Clear useful job, manageable friction | Consequential recurring problem, compelling bounded solution |
| Accuracy | Freedom from consequential errors and misleading claims in the reviewed presentation | Multiple serious misleading claims | A material contradiction or several overclaims | Mostly sound, meaningful qualifications needed | Consistent, well-bounded claims, no material issue in scope |
| Uniqueness | Distinctiveness of the proposition or workflow | Little visible differentiation | Familiar category with useful tailoring | Recognizably differentiated approach | Strongly distinctive, supported by focused comparison |
| Quality | Thoughtfulness and completeness on inspected surfaces | Important workflow or evidence gaps | Useful core, uneven execution | Coherent workflow, credible limits, useful docs | Exceptionally considered boundaries, examples, recovery |
| Clarity | Ease of understanding the job, result, requirements, and next step | Reader must reconstruct the product | Understandable after substantial interpretation | Main job and path are clear, some friction | Immediate understanding, concrete examples, obvious first step |
| Aesthetics | Effectiveness and polish of inspected branding and visual presentation | Visual treatment impairs use or credibility | Functional but inconsistent, dated, or weakly organized | Cohesive, readable, appropriate | Distinctive, polished, makes the result easier to understand |
| Overall appeal | Holistic attractiveness to the stated audience | Weak reason to adopt as presented | Mixed proposition, significant hesitation | Compelling enough to investigate or try | Highly compelling, strong presentation, credible evidence |

Scores 1–2 are severe failure. 4, 6, and 8 sit between anchors.
A 10 needs unusually strong positive evidence and no material
reservation in scope. Do not give a 10 because no problem was noticed.

Accuracy is an inverse presentation-error judgment. Keep that
definition explicit in every scorecard.

## Procedure

1. Write the primary user and job before assigning numbers.
2. Record the strongest positive evidence and material reservations.
3. Select the nearest anchor and justify it in one or two sentences.
4. Assign confidence: high, medium, low. Insufficient → `NR`.
5. Check consistency across similar subjects.
6. Preserve disagreements instead of manufacturing precision.

Do not lower a score merely because confidence is lower.
Overall appeal is not the average of the other six.

Uniqueness is not a proxy for usefulness. Clarity is comprehension.
Aesthetics is the effectiveness of the visual presentation.
